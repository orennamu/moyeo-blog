## Why Gwangmyeong Cave?

**Gwangmyeong Cave (광명동굴)** is not a limestone wonder carved by nature. It is a **man-made mining tunnel**—about **7.8 km** long and up to **275 m** deep—on the southwestern edge of the Seoul metro area in **Gwangmyeong-si, Gyeonggi-do**.

[VISITKOREA](https://english.visitkorea.or.kr/svc/contents/contentsView.do?vcontsId=194429) describes its arc clearly: developed from **1912** under Japanese colonial resource extraction, continued as an industrial mine after liberation, closed in **1972**, then used for roughly **40 years** as a **salted-seafood (saeu-jeot) warehouse**. In **2011**, Gwangmyeong City bought the site and turned industrial heritage into a metropolitan **cave theme park**—lights, sculptures, media art, an aquarium, and an underground wine cellar.

Korean tourism guides (구석구석) call it a landmark of the city, a past **Korea Tourism Star** pick, and a stop on Korea Tourism 100 lists. The pitch is simple: history underground, cool air year-round, and a full afternoon of stops without leaving the capital region.

## A mine that kept changing jobs

구석구석’s long-form feature traces the site’s hard timeline: colonial forced labor and mineral extraction (gold, silver, copper, zinc), postwar industrial mining, shelter use during the Korean War, then a 1972 flood that pushed tailings into farmland and helped shut the mine. Quiet storage years followed—cool tunnels were perfect for fermenting shrimp paste from nearby Sorae markets—until the city reinvented the space as culture tourism.

Only about **2 km** of the tunnel system is open to visitors today (mainly Level 0 and part of underground Level 1). That is enough for light shows, the Cave Art Hall, aquarium tanks filled with cave groundwater, the “King of the Cave” dragon installation, a modern history exhibit, and the famous **Wine Cave** kept near a steady **12°C**.

## How to visit (practical)

| Item | Typical detail |
| --- | --- |
| Address | 142 Gahak-ro 85beon-gil, Gwangmyeong-si, Gyeonggi-do |
| Hours | **09:00–18:00** (last entry **17:00**); closed **Mondays** |
| Time inside | Often **30–90 minutes** walking the open route; many stay longer for shows & wine |

| Fees (VISITKOREA) | Adults **10,000** / teens **5,000** / children **3,000** won (group & resident discounts apply; check the official site) |

**Getting there from Seoul**

- **KTX Gwangmyeong Station** → city bus **17** or **77** to Gwangmyeong Cave  
- **Subway Line 7, Cheolsan Station** → bus **17** toward the cave  

For English help: **1330** Korea Travel Hotline. Cave visitor center: **+82-70-4277-8902**.

Nearby, 구석구석 also points to the **Gwangmyeong Upcycle Art Center**—another reuse story next door—and the old **ore-dressing plant (seongwangjang)** preserved as industrial heritage outside the tunnel.

## When it shines brightest

- **Summer:** The constant cool makes it a classic heat-escape day trip (Gyeonggi tourism often markets it as all-weather).  
- **Rainy days:** The whole program is indoors once you enter.  
- **Families:** Aquarium, light zones, and kid-friendly exhibits; allow extra time for photo stops.  
- **Wine travelers:** Korean regional bottles stored and tasted underground—an odd, memorable pairing with mine history.

Confirm Monday closures and peak-season hour tweaks on the city site before you go.

## A traveler’s takeaway

Gwangmyeong Cave is Korea’s short answer to “what happens when a city refuses to leave a scar underground?” The tunnels still remember colonial extraction and industrial boom years; the lights, wine racks, and media art are the city’s rewrite. For visitors based in Seoul, it is one of the easiest ways to spend a half-day somewhere that feels nowhere near a subway platform—yet is only a bus ride past Gwangmyeong Station.
