Korea Spotlights #2 — Introducing Korea’s landmarks in English.

This is the second stop in a series that introduces Korea’s landmarks in English—this time an all-weather day trip just outside Seoul.

Inside Gwangmyeong Cave. Photo: *Youngjin / Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0

Why Gwangmyeong Cave?

Gwangmyeong Cave (광명동굴) is not a limestone wonder carved by nature. It is a man-made mining tunnel—about 7.8 km long and up to 275 m deep—on the southwestern edge of the Seoul metro area in Gwangmyeong-si, Gyeonggi-do.

VISITKOREA describes its arc clearly: developed from 1912 under Japanese colonial resource extraction, continued as an industrial mine after liberation, closed in 1972, then used for roughly 40 years as a salted-seafood (saeu-jeot) warehouse. In 2011, Gwangmyeong City bought the site and turned industrial heritage into a metropolitan cave theme park—lights, sculptures, media art, an aquarium, and an underground wine cellar.

Korean tourism guides call it a landmark of the city, a past Korea Tourism Star pick, and a stop on Korea Tourism 100 lists. The pitch is simple: history underground, cool air year-round, and a full afternoon of stops without leaving the capital region.

A mine that kept changing jobs

VISITKOREA / 대한민국 구석구석 features trace the site’s hard timeline: colonial forced labor and mineral extraction (gold, silver, copper, zinc), postwar industrial mining, shelter use during the Korean War, then a 1972 flood that helped shut the mine. Quiet storage years followed—cool tunnels were perfect for fermenting shrimp paste—until the city reinvented the space as culture tourism.

Only about 2 km of the tunnel system is open to visitors today (mainly Level 0 and part of underground Level 1). That is enough for light shows, the Cave Art Hall, aquarium tanks filled with cave groundwater, the “King of the Cave” dragon installation, a modern history exhibit, and the famous Wine Cave kept near a steady 12°C.

How to visit (practical)

  • Address: 142 Gahak-ro 85beon-gil, Gwangmyeong-si, Gyeonggi-do
  • Hours: 09:00–18:00 (last entry 17:00); closed Mondays
  • Time inside: often 30–90 minutes on the open route; many stay longer
  • Temperature: roughly 10–13°C year-round — bring a light jacket
  • Fees (VISITKOREA): Adults 10,000 / teens 5,000 / children 3,000 won (discounts apply; confirm on the official site)
  • Official site: gm.go.kr/cv/en

Getting there from Seoul

  • KTX Gwangmyeong Station → city bus 17 or 77 to Gwangmyeong Cave
  • Subway Line 7, Cheolsan Station → bus 17 toward the cave

For English help: 1330 Korea Travel Hotline. Cave visitor center: +82-70-4277-8902.

Nearby, guides also point to the Gwangmyeong Upcycle Art Center and the preserved ore-dressing plant outside the tunnel.

When it shines brightest

  • Summer: constant cool air makes it a classic heat-escape day trip.
  • Rainy days: the program is indoors once you enter.
  • Families: aquarium, light zones, and kid-friendly exhibits.
  • Wine travelers: Korean regional bottles stored and tasted underground.

Confirm Monday closures and peak-season hour tweaks on the city site before you go.

A traveler’s takeaway

Gwangmyeong Cave is Korea’s short answer to “what happens when a city refuses to leave a scar underground?” The tunnels still remember colonial extraction and industrial boom years; the lights, wine racks, and media art are the city’s rewrite. For visitors based in Seoul, it is one of the easiest ways to spend a half-day somewhere that feels nowhere near a subway platform—yet is only a bus ride past Gwangmyeong Station.
