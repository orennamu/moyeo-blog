Why this short stretch of track matters

Gyeongam-dong Railroad Village (경암동 철길마을) sits in Gunsan-si, Jeonbuk State, where houses and tiny shops press almost against a preserved industrial railway. VISITKOREA’s Korean portal (대한민국 구석구석) puts it on a soft Gunsan “emotion trip” with Cho-won Photo Studio and Eunpa Lake Park: a day of film nostalgia, painted alleys, and cherry-blossom waterfronts when the season is right.

The story is industrial, then human. In 1944, during the Japanese colonial period, a freight spur was laid to haul newsprint materials between what became the Paper Korea mill and Gunsan Station—about 2.5 km of track. Workers settled beside the rails; by the 1970s a neighborhood had filled in. Trains still crawled through twice a day into 2008, slow enough for whistles and hand signals, close enough that houses shook as the cars passed. After service ended, the city kept the rails and reinvented the corridor as a “street of memories”—snack stalls, murals, and rental school uniforms instead of freight cars.

Cultural guides often highlight the visitor-facing stretch of roughly 400 m between Jinpo and Yeonan intersections: two-story 1970s houses on one side, low warehouses on the other, sleepers and rails still underfoot. Photographers once came for the surreal sight of a train threading a residential alley; travelers now come for the same geometry, without the train.

What to do on the tracks

구석구석’s seasonal feature is blunt about the vibe: little renovation theater—1970s–80s textures left in place. Along the rails you will find:

  • Nostalgia snacks and play: dalgona candy, ppopgi (drawing lottery), ddakji, dried fish snacks, and other “bad snacks” of childhood memory
  • Costume rentals: period school uniforms (and sometimes other retro outfits) for photos on the ties
  • Murals and photo props: cargo-train scenes, flower walls, leapfrog sculptures, a mock train car near the old-station mood zone

Accessible tourism notes (열린관광) list the site as a 2021 Open Tourism pick: tactile/voice information boards, improved approach paths and fencing, nearby accessible parking, and a newer restroom block. The approach section is easier to walk; inside the village, watch your step on rails and uneven ground.

English round-ups match the Korean sources: free entry, open year-round, and a half-day that pairs cleanly with Gunsan’s wider modern-history / time-travel zone.

How to visit (practical)

  • Address: 14 Gyeongchon 4-gil, Gunsan-si, Jeonbuk State (전북특별자치도 군산시 경촌4길 14)
  • Hours: open all year (outdoor street; shops set their own hours)
  • Fee: free entry
  • Parking: nearby public lots
  • Inquiry: +82-63-454-3349
  • City tourism: gunsan.go.kr/tour

Getting there (rough guide)

  • From Gunsan Station / Gunsan Bus Terminal: short taxi ride, or city buses that stop near Lotte Mall Gunsan and a short walk (confirm locally)
  • Pair with Cho-won Photo Studio and Eunpa Lake Park for the full 구석구석 emotion-loop day

English help nationwide: 1330 Korea Travel Hotline.

When it works best

  • Weekday mornings: weekends pack the alley; empty rails photograph better early
  • Film and photo travelers: murals, costumes, and the “houses against the track” frame
  • Families: snack culture and soft nostalgia without a ticket booth
  • Accessibility-aware visitors: use the improved approach; still treat rails as trip hazards

Hours for individual shops change; confirm costume-rental prices on site.

A traveler’s takeaway

Gyeongam-dong is not a theme-park reconstruction bolted onto empty land. It is a working neighborhood that kept its freight spine after the trains stopped—then invited visitors to walk the same corridor workers once cleared for paper rolls. For an English-language traveler, it is one of the clearest ways to feel Gunsan’s modern layer: colonial logistics, 1970s housing density, and today’s selfie economy sharing one set of rails.
